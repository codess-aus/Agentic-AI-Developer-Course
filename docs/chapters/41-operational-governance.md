# Operational governance

Governance drifts when required checks change, ownership becomes stale, permissions grow, or integrations outlive their purpose. Treat controls as a maintained system.

## Set a review cadence

Use a cadence suited to your risk. As a starting point, review failures and repeated policy violations weekly; permissions and secret scopes monthly; and rulesets, owners, environment reviewers, and evidence retention quarterly.

Assign every agent or automation an owner, supported purpose, runbook, and retirement procedure. Reassess access after tool, model, environment, or task-scope changes. Retire unused integrations and credentials.

Do not solve recurring failures by disabling checks. Classify the cause, improve the responsible mechanism, and validate the result.

## Capstone: design a governed contribution

Bring together your task contract, architecture, tool inventory, boundary statement, workflow graph, state handoff, acceptance evidence, and recovery runbook. Walk another person through the dependency-remediation scenario, including a failed check and a late scope change.

!!! success "Workshop complete"
    Your system can explain its goal, bound its actions, preserve progress, prove its result, and stop safely. People and configured policy remain accountable for acceptance.
